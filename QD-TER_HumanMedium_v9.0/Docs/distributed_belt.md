

The Distributed Belt: A Companion Note to v9.0 Part IV

Phase-Locked Group Architecture, Constraint Scaling, and Load Distribution

Status: Public note. Companion to QD-TER_HumanMedium_v9.0. Anchored to §6.6. Claims labeled Conjecture per Theory/CLAIMS.md.

What this note is. v9.0 §6 established the sustained-condition mechanism by which collective coordination produces persistent physiological change. This note formalizes the operational architecture of a phase-locked group — the Distributed Belt — and specifies the constraint-scaling rule under collective coupling. All claims are labeled Conjecture with falsification conditions.

---

Section 1: The Individual Ceiling

Part II §1.2.2 established the biological constraint unit: 3 interfaces × 3 load categories = 9. The single-organism ceiling is 9 constraints.

Attempting to sustain higher constraint satisfaction within a single organism produces internal feedback noise that drives the system toward the Valley (structural ossification) or the Abyss (decoherence). The intra-organism engine-lock cannot maintain additional invariants without metabolic overload.

Part II §1.2.2 also stated the scaling rule:

9 \to 15 \to 25 \to 42,

and stated that these higher scales are not achievable by single organisms. They require engineered substrates or multi-organism coupling.

The Distributed Belt is the multi-organism pathway.

Section 2: The Scaling Rule Under Collective Coupling

The scaling rule applies to the collective:

Constraint count Configuration Status
9 Single organism Biological ceiling for individuals
15 Two-organism phase-lock First collective level; requires cardiac + cephalic coupling
25 Small group (3–5) with stable protocols Sustained collective level; requires all three binding protocols
42 Larger coordinated group Upper near-term collective level; requires long-duration protocol adherence

Each level requires the constraints of the previous level to be maintained. The scaling is multiplicative in the invariant subspace, not additive. Two organisms phase-locked at 15 do not simply add constraints; they multiply the available invariant subspace by a factor determined by the coupling.

This is the same reason 9 rather than 4 or 6 is the base unit: three brains is the minimum for geometric closure, information closure, and observable closure. Two-organism coupling (3 + 3 = 6 oscillators) forms the resonant hexad of Part II §3.5.4. Higher coupling extends the pattern.

Claim C6. Collective constraint satisfaction can exceed the individual 9-ceiling under the three binding protocols of docs/collective_antenna.md §3.

Status. Conjecture.

Section 3: The Mechanism

The mechanism is not transfer of information between nodes. It is the sustained-condition pathway of v9.0 §6:

1. The group's sustained physiological condition (measured via group Re_ε, cardiac coherence, and inter-brain iCOH) establishes a different local environment for each node's chromatin system.
2. The sustained-condition mechanism (v9.0 §6.1) means that a node operating within a coherent group is exposed to a different endocrine pulse geometry than a node operating in isolation.
3. Over time, this different geometry produces a different chromatin baseline through the ordinary §2–§3 mechanism.

The belt does not clear specific ancestral conditions. It changes the sustained condition under which each node's chromatin system operates. The clearing, if it occurs, is the ordinary consequence of the endocrine-chromatin mechanism operating on a different input.

Section 4: Load Distribution

An isolated node carrying deep methylation load at NR3C1 or FKBP5 is pinned to a lower coherence level by the metabolic cost of rewriting that residue (v9.0 §4.5). The cost is concentrated in the node; the available ATP is concentrated in the node; the balance is precarious.

In a phase-locked belt, the load is distributed. The Clearing Matrix formula:

\chi_{\text{group-net}} = \sum \left( \chi_{\text{inherited}} + \chi_{\text{current-load}} \right) - \mathcal{R}_{\text{operator}}\left(\prod \kappa_i^{\text{eff}}\right)

reads: the group's net inherited-plus-current load, minus the ℛ-operator's recovery capacity multiplied across the phase-locked engines. Because the ℛ-operator term is a product over engines, and each engine's effective capacity is amplified by cross-node coupling, the recovery capacity grows faster than the load in a phase-locked array.

Claim C7. The group ℛ-operator recovery capacity grows faster than group load in a phase-locked array, producing net reduction over time.

Status. Conjecture. The mechanism is the sustained-condition pathway of v9.0 §6, not a transfer of specific chromatin marks between individuals.

Section 5: What the Belt Is Not

· Not a supra-human intelligence. The belt produces collective constraint satisfaction past the individual ceiling, not capabilities outside the physics of the constituent nodes. The physical intuition is the antenna array: N elements produce gain and directivity proportional to N, but they do not produce signals the individual elements could not in principle produce. The array amplifies; it does not create.
· Not a mechanism for clearing specific ancestral memories. What propagates across generations is a sustained physiological condition, not content (v9.0 §6.3). What the belt can change is the sustained condition, not the historical events that shaped the parental generation.
· Not a substitute for individual regulation. A node in the Valley cannot be pulled to the Ridge by the group alone. The bifurcation (v9.0 §4.5) is a local metabolic condition. Collective coupling creates the conditions under which individual recovery becomes affordable; it does not perform the recovery.
· Not a ritual. The mechanism is material, not ceremonial. Coordinated research that produces material improvements, shared practice that produces shared physiological conditions, and distributed metabolic load — these are the pathways (v9.0 §6.4). There is no additional mechanism implied by the belt architecture beyond what v9.0 §6 specifies.
· Not a fixed topology. The belt is a configuration of the mechanism, not a specific group size or structure. A dyad can operate as a minimal belt; larger groups extend the same architecture.

Section 6: Falsification Conditions

F5: Collective ceiling. A phase-locked group of 3–5 organisms should sustain constraint satisfaction above the individual 9-ceiling, measured as combined engine-lock and phase-lock coherence, under the three binding protocols.

F6: Load distribution. At least one group member with high individual NR3C1 methylation should show measurable change in methylation pattern over the protocol duration, without individual-level intervention beyond the group context.

F7: φ-scaled coherence at N > 2. Maximal inter-brain coherence should occur at φ-scaled beat frequencies for N > 2, extending Protocol 68 beyond dyads.

F8: Protocol necessity. Removing any one of the three binding protocols should reduce the group's sustainable constraint count below the next lower level (15, 25, or 42).

Each condition is executable with existing EEG hyperscanning, HRV, microbiome assay, and methylation assay methods.
